## ACCELERATE UCONN: NSF I-CORPS PROGRAM

_**Open to: Students, Faculty, Researchers, Staff, Alumni, Community Members**_

Accelerate UConn is the university's [National Science Foundation Innovation Corps](https://icorpsnortheasthub.org/) (I-Corps**™**) program, designed to help teams turn promising scientific and technological concepts into viable ventures. Through this hands-on experience, you’ll dive into the core elements of the I-Corps curriculum and Lean Launchpad methodology — learning how to test assumptions, understand your customer, and refine your idea with real feedback. With guidance and support every step of the way, you’ll gain firsthand experience in entrepreneurship while exploring how your research, innovation, or discovery can make the leap from concept to marketplace.

### FAQs + NSF INFO

All applications will be reviewed by a panel of industry experts. Final selections will be made within a week of the application deadline, and all applicants will be notified of the decision. Final program admission decisions will be announced publicly during program dates.

### Who Should Apply?

Accelerate UConn is open to UConn faculty, postdoctoral researchers, graduate students, and university alumni who are working on a scientific or technical discovery with commercial potential. We also encourage applications from local entrepreneurs, startup founders, and community members who have a technology or innovation they’d like to learn how to commercialize. If you’re ready to explore the market potential of your idea, this program is for you!

### Entrepreneurial Team

**Each team that applies should consist of:**

**Entrepreneurial Lead (EL):** The EL is responsible for driving the process of customer discovery and business model development, and may also recruit additional people to participate in the team. On faculty-led teams, the EL is typically a postdoctoral researcher, graduate student, or undergraduate student, who possesses the relevant technical knowledge and a deep commitment to investigate the commercial landscape surrounding the innovation, and is responsible for leading the development of the business model for the product or technology proposed. On student, alumni or community member led teams, the EL is the member who acts as the point person during the length of the program, makes presentations on behalf of the group, and takes responsibility for group performance.

**Technical Lead (TL):** The TL is a faculty member that has led the development of a new product or technology, or has relevant knowledge about the problem the venture will solve. This person can act as the team’s Chief Technology Officer or advisor, and could also choose to take a more involved role in the development of the business strategy. On student, alumni or community member led teams, the TL does not need to be a faculty member, but rather is the member who has led the development of the product or technology, or has relevant knowledge about the problem the venture will solve.

**Optional –**

**Industry Mentor:** An Industry Mentor is typically someone with experience transitioning technology out of academic labs and into the marketplace, or with experience bringing new products to market within the industry that the team is trying to enter.

Are you looking for an Entrepreneurial Lead for your team? Reach out to alycia.chrosniak@uconn.edu. Interested in being an Entrepreneurial Lead for an existing team? [Apply here.](https://ccei.uconn.edu/entrepreneurial-lead-application/)

### Selection Criteria

**Problem Definition:**Does the team have a clear and broad understanding of the problem their product or technology seeks to solve? Have they clearly articulated the market or societal need they are filling? Is there a clear pain-point that their product is addressing?

**Strength of Team**: Does the team include the diversity of expertise necessary to accomplish its goals? Has the team engaged a strong group of advisors and/or partners? Does the team make a convincing presentation of the need for their product, and their dedication to the process of creating a new venture?

**Technical Feasibility:**Does the product or technology have the capability to produce the desired outcome or benefit? Is it technologically feasible? Can it be produced on a scale that will meet demand and solve the problem intended?

**Innovation & Novelty:**Does the product demonstrate the potential to significantly improve a process or system? Is the product or technology fundamentally different from existing solutions?

### Timing + Commitments

**What to expect:**
*   The EL and TL must attend and represent their team at all in-person and virtual meetings during the program. This is approximately a 15 hour commitment over the four weeks.
*   Outside of meeting times, an additional 5-8 hours should be dedicated weekly to customer discovery and the recording of information gained through interviews.
*   Each team is expected to complete a minimum of 20 customer discovery interviews by the end of the four weeks.
*   Each team is expected to attend a minimum of two, 30 minute office hour sessions with their pod mentor.

In 2024, UConn joined the NSF Innovation Corps (I-Corps TM) Northeast Hub.

The Northeast Hub is part of a nationwide NSF-funded network of universities formed to accelerate the economic impact of federally funded research – delivering benefits in health care, the environment, technology and other areas – while building skills and opportunities among researchers from all backgrounds, including those historically underrepresented in entrepreneurship. As part of the hub, UConn joins nine partner institutions including: Princeton University, University of Delaware, Rutgers University, Lehigh University, Temple University, New Jersey Institute of Technology (NJIT), Rowan University, Delaware State University, Drexel University and Yale University.
